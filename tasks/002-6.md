# 002-6: the line and the daily processor, one draw at a time, stepping from any day to any later day

The product is moving to an exact evaluator (`design/002-exact-evaluator.md`): instead of sampling the lawsuit's
future, it walks the whole tree of what can happen, one tree per operating draw, depth first, in compiled code
(numba), carrying the state down the tree and saving and restoring it at each decision instead of re-running a
history from the review date. That is what makes it fast enough to be exact on a laptop: today's Python walk re-runs
the loan engine over the whole horizon for every cash read, and that is a large part of why it needs about 1,500
core-hours where an hour on 8 cores is the target.

This task is the first component: the loan line and the daily cash processor for ONE draw, as a state that can be
advanced from day a to day b given the event cash on those days, copied cheaply, and read at any point.

Today `engine.run` (`app/analysis/engine.py`) runs every draw over the whole horizon at once; under daily processing
(the Akoustis case: `cash_processing = daily`, `collection = debit`) it goes through `processor.run_daily` and the
compiled `processor._daily_kernel`; `events.Chain.processed` and `_daily_run` are how the event chain reads it (the
end-of-day available cash, the first unpaid day, the §3.3 general-nonpayment day, the arrears by class). Read both
modules whole before changing anything: their module docstrings state the rules (limit, draws, collections in
due-date order, debits in full or not at all, the opening exposure, the petition, arrears classes and the order
obligations are cleared).

Write, in a new module `app/analysis/line_state.py`, compiled with numba (nopython, `cache=True`):
- a per-draw state (fixed-size arrays and scalars: what the daily kernel carries from one day to the next for one
  draw: balances, the due schedule and outstanding installments with their draw order, arrears by class with their
  incurred days, the trailing receipts the limit reads, outstanding principal, collected and funded so far, the
  petition, the first unpaid day, the nonpayment test's window), created at the review date from the line and the
  opening exposure;
- `advance(state, line tables for this draw, event cash for this draw, a, b)`: run days a..b-1 exactly as the daily
  kernel would, writing per-day outputs (cash, collections, fundings, due, outstanding, locked, capacity, headroom
  rows) for those days into caller-provided arrays;
- a copy that costs a few microseconds, so a caller can save the state at a decision and restore it per answer.

What must hold, shown on the real case: for any event cash (use real ones: `events.event_trace` on histories from a
small walk of the Akoustis 14 May case, as `tests/test_continuation.py` and `tests/test_stress_search.py` build
them), running one draw from day 0 to the horizon in any split into segments (0..a, a..b, b..horizon, with copies
taken and restored between) gives exactly the trajectories `engine.run` gives for that draw, every day, every field
the event chain and the analysis read (the daily cash, collections, fundings, due, outstanding, the first unpaid day,
the nonpayment day, arrears by class, stayed, preference, not yet due, the headroom rows). Exactly: integers equal.
Report the time per draw-day of `advance` and per copy, measured on this machine (single core, after compilation).
The evaluator's budget is about 100 million history-draws, each advancing a few weeks between decisions, in an hour
on 8 cores: so `advance` should cost well under a microsecond per draw-day, and a copy a few microseconds; if that is
out of reach, say what costs it.

Keep to the new module and its tests; do not change engine.py or processor.py (the Python stays the definition while
both exist). Net processing (`cash_processing = net`) is not needed now; say if anything in the daily rules depends
on state the per-draw record cannot hold.

Done: the per-draw state, `advance` and the copy, identical to `engine.run` on real event cash for every split
tested, with the measured speed.
