# 001-15a: how concentrated is the tree's probability?

The product forecasts a borrower's loan cash under a judgment model's answers to dated questions about a lawsuit. So
far it walks every path under every possible answer, across 512 cash draws, before any answer is known: for the
Akoustis case about 8 million joint histories and thousands of core-hours. Owen has decided to replace this with an
answer-first walk: the judgment model answers questions as they are reached, branches are expanded in order of
probability, and the walk stops when the unexpanded probability on every draw is below a tolerance, with every page
figure reported as an interval whose width that remainder bounds, and the worst feasible path found by its own search.
Whether that is fast depends on one fact nobody has measured: how much of each draw's probability sits in how few
histories. That number decides the tolerance Owen will choose and whether the design is worth building.

Measure it on the histories already walked, with no model calls. Each history's probability on a draw is the product of
its answers' probabilities on that draw (`path_probability` with `path_mask`, after `expand_classes`, as the pool's
export does in `app/analysis/tree_pool.py`). Real answers do not exist yet, so bracket them:
- the pool's random answer distributions (`random_dist`, seeds 17, 91, 307);
- skewed distributions, where each question gives one answer (chosen at random, seeded) 80% and, separately, 95% of
  its probability and splits the rest, standing in for a judgment model that is fairly or very sure.
For each distribution and each of the 512 draws: the probability the walked histories carry, and how many histories,
taken in order of probability, reach 90%, 99%, 99.9% and 99.99% of it. Report the distribution of those counts over
draws (median, 90th percentile, worst) and of the history-draws they imply.

Which histories:
- the walk on the current code, walk-h1 and walk-h2 (GitHub runs 38271466884 and 38273580489; about
  179,000 histories), and
- for scale, waves walk-f1 to walk-f41 (about 6 million histories; run IDs in `.runs/remote-walk-f*.log`; the AWS
  halves of f7-f19 are not on GitHub, so leave them out and say so). That walk's code had a fact leak since fixed;
  the tree's shape is close enough for this measurement, and its larger size shows the tail.
The walks are partial, so the walked probability per draw is below 1: report it, and treat the counts as reaching a
share of what was walked as well as of 1.

The pieces are GitHub artifacts (`out-N` manifests, `heavy-N` events; the pool's readers already pair them). This is
heavy: put it in `var/diag/001-15a/run.txt`, in lines that each finish within 20 minutes and write a small mergeable
summary (for example, per draw and distribution, probability and count by fine log-probability bin), with one line
that merges them. The coordinator runs it and returns the outputs to you only if you ask in your report; so make the
merge line write the final tables to `var/diag/001-15a/` itself.
