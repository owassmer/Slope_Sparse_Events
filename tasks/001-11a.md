# 001-11a What bounding the recurring decisions would cut, and what it would change

One draw's tree from the review date is large mostly because some decisions recur on a path
(`var/diag/001-6b/report.md`): settlement asked again at each later legal stage (up to a fifth ask), the cash-floor
decision at each new fall below the operating need, an offering started again after a failed one, the notes' second
default decision, listing. The full walk would take some 4-6 hours of runner time. Owen will decide whether to bound
some of these, as declared scenarios; this task measures each candidate so he can.

Candidates, each with what happens instead once it applies:
1. Settlement asked at no more than three legal stages; after the third, no further offer.
2. The cash-floor decision on no more than two occasions; at later falls, the company does nothing further at the floor.
3. No more than two offerings started within the horizon; no third.
4. Any other recurrence the attribution shows to be large, with an equally plain default (say which and why).

For each candidate alone and for all together, measure on the heavy draws (146, 365, 438) and two light ones (73, 511):
- the tree size and walk time it leaves (the existing Knuth estimator, with the bound applied);
- the share of histories and draws it touches;
- what it changes for the lender, from walking the same draws with and without it on a bounded sample: the
  collections by 10 Nov, the filing probability by 10 Nov and the balance at filing, per touched history and in the
  aggregate under uniform answers and under a few other explicit synthetic answer sets (not model judgments).
A bound must be a switch, off by default, so the walk without it is unchanged. Put the runs in `run.txt`, each line
within 20 minutes. Report in `var/` and your final message, plainly: what each bound saves and what it costs the
page's numbers. No cloud and no judgment calls.
