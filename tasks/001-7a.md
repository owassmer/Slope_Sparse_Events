# 001-7a Where finishing a history spends its time, and whether draws walk better together

A Knuth attribution of the chronological walk (`var/diag/001-6b/report.md`) found terminal emission, finishing each
completed history, takes 85-90% of a heavy draw's walk time. The full tree is about 1,900 CPU-hours over 512 draws
(`var/remote/001-5c/`), so this is where the time goes.

Measure, on one heavy draw from the review date for a few minutes:
1. What finishing a history spends its time on, split into: replaying the path (from scratch, or continuing from the
   state the walk already holds at the leaf), the tail from the last decision to the horizon (cash processing and
   Slope's line), re-recording deferred questions, building traces and rows, and handing the history on. Say which
   parts repeat work the walk has already done, and which are needed only by the reduction.
2. How many histories end in the same terminal state (the state found sufficient in `var/diag/001-5d/report.md`:
   cash and obligations, pending decisions and dates, committed date, stays, listing), so their tail could be computed
   once.
3. The cost per history-draw of walking one draw alone against walking a population of draws together (the walk can
   carry several draws and split them only where decision orders differ), on the same draws, for example 8 and 64 of
   them, for the same few minutes.

Report the numbers and what each implies for the full tree, in your final message, with data in `var/`. Measure only;
change no product behaviour. No cloud and no judgment calls.
