# 002-2: the worst feasible path the walk does not reach, and a bound no path exceeds

The product shows a lender how a lawsuit could move a borrower's loan: collections, timing and exposure under a
judgment model's answers. Outcome 002 (`outcomes/002.md`) builds the page from an answer-first walk
(`design/002-answer-first.md`, `app/disputes/answer_first.py`): the likely histories are walked exactly and the rest
sampled. That leaves unlikely histories unwalked, and among them can be the worst ones for the lender. Removing a
feasible stress path because it is unlikely is a critical error here (AGENTS.md). So the page needs, beside its
probability-weighted figures, the worst feasible history found without regard to probability, and a bound that no
feasible history can exceed, so the reader knows how far the worst found could be from the worst there is.

The measure of "worst" is the page's existing stress ranking (`app/analysis/core.py`: `stress_rows`, `_stress_row`;
`app/analysis/page.py`: `_worst`): unrecovered at maturity (stayed plus uncollected, mean over the draws), then the
claim that would be stayed by a petition on the day of the highest expected outstanding balance. It is computed per
history, unweighted.

The bound. Read the line's rules in `app/analysis/engine.py` (module docstring, `limits`, the draw condition in the
collection loop): a new draw is funded only while principal outstanding plus the invoice stays within that day's
limit, and the limit is computed from the trajectory's operating receipts and debt service, which no event path
changes. Principal outstanding is funded less collections times funded over contractual, so what is owed is principal
outstanding times contractual over funded. If that holds as it reads, then on each draw what is owed at any time, and
so what can be unrecovered at maturity or stayed at a petition, is at most the larger of the opening exposure's
remaining contractual balance and (1 + fee) times the highest limit over the horizon. Confirm or correct this against
the code (including opening exposure, rounding and anything else that can raise what is owed), and compute the bound
per draw and its mean. Show by a test, over every history of a walked tree on a few draws, that no history's
unrecovered-at-maturity exceeds the bound on any draw.

The search. Find a feasible history as bad as you can by that ranking, ignoring probability: every answer must be
one its question offers on its date, which holds for any history the chronological walk emits (`ChronologicalWalk`
in `app/disputes/chronological.py`; `AnswerFirstWalk` shows how to sample children inside it with a guide). A full
search of the tree is out of reach (millions of histories), so search: for example sample many histories with every
offered answer equally likely, evaluated on a subset of draws, keep the worst, improve it one answer at a time, and
evaluate the winner on all 512 draws. It must run inside `slope run` (`app/cli.py`, `app/analysis/build.py`) in about
ten minutes on this machine, and put in the page payload (`page_payload` in `app/analysis/page.py`) a `stress` block:
the worst history found (its sequence in the page's words, as `_worst` gives it, and its figures), how it was searched
(histories evaluated, draws), and the bound per the measure above. Say on the page payload, in plain words, that the
search found it and the bound is what no history can exceed; it is not claimed to be the worst there is.

Everything runs locally and quickly: the real case's context is `app/analysis/population_walk.context()`; keep
draws few while searching. No model calls. Done: the bound is confirmed against the engine and tested over a walked
tree; `slope run akoustis_20240514` writes the `stress` block in its page.json within about ten more minutes.
