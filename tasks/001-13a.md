# 001-13a: how much of the full walk is left

The full-tree walk (`slope walk-tree`, all 512 operating draws walked together, in 15-minute pieces that hand on their
unfinished routes) has run nine waves on the final code. About 1.5M joint histories and 20.7M history-draws are done.
Each wave finishes only about one piece in six, and the count of unfinished routes keeps growing (about 3,600 after
wave f8). That count says nothing about how much work is left: a route near the root can hold most of the tree. A
paid AWS host runs beside GitHub's runners within a fixed budget, so the decision to keep going, and for how long,
needs an estimate of the work that remains, with its uncertainty.

Make the walk able to say how much is left under a piece's unfinished routes, and say it for the walk as it stands.
- Use the same kind of unbiased random-probe (Knuth) estimate as `slope estimate-walk`, but started from a piece's
  remaining routes rather than from the review date. The walk's own rules apply, including the production scenario the
  pieces were walked under (`cases/akoustis_20240514/scenario.json`) and every draw walked together, so the estimate
  counts what the walk will actually do: joint histories and history-draws, and the walking time they imply.
- Report the remaining total with a spread that comes from the probes themselves, and the pieces that hold most of it.
- The continuation states of the wave now running are in `var/waves/walk-f9/walk-resume/walk-f9/*/piece.json`. The
  outputs of earlier waves, including each piece's final `histories` and `history_draws`, are under `var/remote/`
  (`walk-fN/` from GitHub, `walk-fN.aws/` from AWS); `var/waves/walk-fN/` holds the states each wave started from.
- Before trusting it, check the estimator where the answer is known: pieces that started in an earlier wave and
  finished in that same wave (one share, `complete` true) give the exact count below their starting state. The
  estimates for those should agree with the actual counts within the stated spread (a 90% interval should hold the
  actual count for about nine pieces in ten), across many such pieces, not one.
- The estimate for all of walk-f9's continuations must finish within one 20-minute runner line (GitHub's runners stop
  each line at 20 minutes, and the answer is needed before the next waves are paid for); put that command in
  `run.txt` (split it across lines if it needs more), writing its result as it goes. Small checks on the finished
  pieces can run here.

Done: a command that estimates the remaining work below a set of continuation states, shown to agree with known
counts on finished pieces, and a `run.txt` line that runs it on walk-f9's continuations.
