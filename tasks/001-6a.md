# 001-6a The chronological walk walks each state's future once

One draw's tree from the review date is about 120,000 histories and 80 minutes of walking (`var/diag/001-5c/`);
over 512 draws that is far too slow. A measurement (`var/diag/001-5d/report.md`) found that histories reaching the same
state share their whole future, where the state is: event cash and obligations (with incurred days, proceeds, locks,
capacity, petition), the pending decisions with their dates (the walk's unresolved cursors and frontier dates), the
committed date, the dated stay records (motion and approval dates and status), and the dated listing status. Walk
labels (last response, late records, answer history) did not distinguish futures.

Make the chronological walk, within one draw, walk each such state's future once: a history that reaches a state
already walked links to it instead of walking it again. The result is a graph of states joined by answered questions
and deterministic steps, from which every history can be expanded. Probabilities stay a product of answers along each
history. Nothing about questions, answers or booking changes.

Exact check, on a few small native roots and on the review root for a few minutes: expanding the graph gives the same
histories, the same cash per history and the same question keys and facts as the plain walk; random expanded histories
pass the no-skip and no-future checks. If two merged states turn out to have different futures, the key is missing
something: find the field and add it. Report histories, distinct states and time against the plain walk on the same
roots. Put several review-date draws in `run.txt`, each within 20 minutes, writing as they go. No cloud and no
judgment calls.
