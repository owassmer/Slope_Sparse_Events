# 002-9: a question's record, compiled for one draw

The product is moving to an exact evaluator (`design/002-exact-evaluator.md`; read it whole, especially "The dated
walk" and "The walk, compiled"): the whole tree of what can happen in the lawsuit, walked one operating draw at a time
in numba. Every question the judgment model answers is identified by its key and its situation class, and is put to
the model with the facts of its decision day (its record). The walk's first pass collects, for every question asked
anywhere in the tree, its records on each draw; the classes decide which questions are distinct, and the records are
what the page and the judge read. Today the Python builds a record as a dict (`events.Chain.question_row`,
`c_situation`); the compiled walk needs the same record as numbers it can take at each decision without leaving numba.

What exists to build on (read each, and what it calls):
- `app/exact/agenda.py`, `replay.py`: one draw's state (`St`, `K`) and a history's steps booked on it as
  `Chain.advance` books them; `app/exact/walk.py`: the walk's scheduler, which follows a history of the Python walk
  (`follow`) choosing each decision at its own date.
- The compiled accessors the record reads: `app/exact/core.py` (amount owed, live), `cash.py` (the day's cash,
  `processing_balance`, the cash triggers), `equity.py` (share price, ledger, offering terms and availability, option
  groups), `notes.py` (the default, the standing, the route), `agenda.py` (`collateral_before`, `pending_levy`,
  `settlement_window`, `project`: the projected settlement and security terms).
- The Python: `Chain.question_row`, `c_situation`, `c_read` and every accessor it names, `trigger_days`, `marks_now`,
  the step's offers (`settle_offer`, `stay_offer`, `raise_offer`), `security_terms`, `settlement_pricing`;
  `forecast.as_of`, `situation_class`, `_Walk.contexts`, `context_class`, `group_classes`; and
  `app/disputes/state14.py`, which renders a record for the judge (what it reads is what must be right).

Write `app/exact/fields_record.py` (the record's fixed layout: integer and float columns, each naming the Python
field it holds; strings as codes with their vocabularies), `app/exact/record.py` (a compiled `record(st, k, day, node,
ctx, ...)` writing one record row from the draw's state as it stands; and a compiled situation class as integer codes,
the same partition as `situation_class` with the context tags and the option group), `app/exact/decode.py` (Python: a
record row back into the dict `question_row` returns, so `as_of`, `situation_class` and `state14` read it unchanged; the
class codes back into the class string) and `tests/test_exact_record.py`.

What must hold, on random real histories (`oracle.histories`, both styles, several seeds, a few draws), driven through
the compiled scheduler as `walk.follow` drives them (copy its loop into the test, taking the record at each decision
before it books: after `agenda.until(day)`, and for a step that does not wait, after `agenda.prepare`): each decoded
record equals the Python's record of that step (`Chain.run` with `capture_questions`, `Trace.questions[i]`, after
`as_of`), field by field, on the draw; and the decoded class equals the Python's class for it (what the walk records
in `DisputePath.classes`, or `situation_class` with the context and group on the Python's row). Floats exactly as the
Python computes them. Where they differ, find why: the compiled walk takes each record from the state on its own date
(the design's "The dated walk" lists where the Python reads another state: a court's question, a deferred record, a
projection); report each difference by its cause with an example, and do not make the compiled record copy a Python
read that sees a later answer. Report what you checked (histories, draws, records by node, fields) and every
difference class.

Keep to your new files; do not change `events.py`, `agenda.py`, `walk.py`, `replay.py`, `fields*.py` of other areas
or the other areas' modules (the coordinator is changing the walk at the same time). If you need something from them,
say so in your report.
