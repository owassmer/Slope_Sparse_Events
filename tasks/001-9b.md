# 001-9b A continuation can take a share of the remaining branches

`slope walk-tree --resume <piece.json>` continues an unfinished piece of the full-tree walk: it walks all of the
piece's remaining branches (its antichain) in one process. After the first wave, 28 unfinished pieces held 771
remaining branches, so the heavy pieces continue one process each while most of GitHub's runner capacity sits idle,
and the walk would take some 30 waves.

Let a continuation take a share of a piece's remaining branches, for example `--share i/k`: share i of k, the k shares
together covering every remaining branch exactly once, so k continuations of one piece can run in parallel. Keep each
share's output a complete, well-formed piece (its own `piece.json`, and its own `next.txt` if it does not finish), so
the coverage check can tell that the shares of a piece together walked each branch once. Show it locally on the
saved unfinished piece in `var/diag/001-7d/partition63/piece.json`: k shares walked separately produce exactly the
histories the unsplit continuation produces. No cloud and no judgment calls.
