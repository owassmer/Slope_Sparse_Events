## R2a: Third Circuit remittitur standard

**Citations:**
- *Kazan v. Wolinski*, 721 F.2d 911, 914 (3d Cir. 1983): a court may order the plaintiff "to remit the portion of the verdict in excess of the maximum amount supportable by the evidence or, if the remittitur is refused, to submit to a new trial." Quoted in *Feuerstein v. Simpson*, No. 04-134 (D.V.I. 2013).
- *Cortez v. Trans Union, LLC*, 617 F.3d 688, 715–16 (3d Cir. 2010): conditional remittitur is appropriate when the verdict is "clearly unsupported" or excessive; review is for abuse of discretion. Followed in *Dee v. Borough of Dunmore* (3d Cir. 2012).
- *Gumbs v. Pueblo Int'l, Inc.*, 823 F.2d 768 (3d Cir. 1987).
- *Evans v. Port Auth.*, 273 F.3d 346 (3d Cir. 2001): the district court remitted to "the maximum award that a reasonable jury could impose".
- *Spence v. Bd. of Educ.*, 806 F.2d 1198 (3d Cir. 1986).
- *Hetzel v. Prince William County*, 523 U.S. 208 (1998) (per curiam): the Seventh Amendment requires that a plaintiff be offered a new trial rather than a reduced award imposed without consent.
- Regional-circuit law governs remittitur on the patent component.

**Answer.** The Third Circuit applies a maximum-recovery approach. If the award is clearly unsupported or grossly excessive, the court fixes the highest amount the evidence supports. Qorvo then chooses between accepting that amount and a new trial on damages; it cannot be forced to accept a reduction (*Hetzel*).

Akoustis asks for remittitur "based on the unjust enrichment calculation submitted by Akoustis' damages expert in the amount of $305,000" (D.I. 613 ¶1). That is the *defense* number. Under a maximum-recovery rule the court would not remit to the minimum. The more likely landing points are:
- the highest head-start figure the admitted evidence supports: Appendix C runs month by month from 1 to 55 months (see R5); or
- a new trial on damages, which D.I. 613 also requests on inconsistent-verdict and *Daubert* grounds.

An accepted remittitur ends the damages dispute at the trial level. A refusal leads to a retrial.

**Disposition:** SETTLED on the standard. OPEN on whether this court finds the award excessive, and on the number it would fix.

## R2b: N.C. punitive cap, DTSA exemplary cap, and the $7.0M award

**Citations:**
- N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1D-25(b): "Punitive damages awarded against a defendant shall not exceed three times the amount of compensatory damages or two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000), whichever is greater." The court reduces any excess.
- N.C. Gen. Stat. § 66-154(c): "If willful and malicious misappropriation exists, the trier of fact also may award punitive damages in its discretion."
- § 66-154(b): actual damages are "measured by the economic loss or the unjust enrichment ... whichever is greater."
- 18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)(3)(C): exemplary damages "not more than 2 times the amount of the damages awarded under subparagraph (B)."
- *GE Betz, Inc. v. Conrad*, 231 N.C. App. 214 (2013): applied the § 1D-25(b) maximum to NCTSPA punitive damages.

**Answer.** NCTSPA punitive damages are "punitive damages" under Chapter 1D, so the § 1D-25 cap applies (*GE Betz*). Because § 66-154(b) calls unjust enrichment "actual damages", the $31.3M is the compensatory base. The caps work out as follows:
- N.C. cap: $93.9M.
- DTSA cap: $62.6M.
- Award: $7.0M, a ratio of 0.22×, well inside both caps.

A constitutional (*State Farm*) ratio challenge would also be weak at a sub-1:1 ratio.

**Disposition:** SETTLED.

## R2c: Permanent-injunction standards and design-around

**Citations:**
- 18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)(3)(A): a court "may" grant an injunction "to prevent any actual or threatened misappropriation ... on such terms as the court deems reasonable". The order may not bar employment. It may condition future use on a royalty "in exceptional circumstances" (§ 1836(b)(3)(A)(iii)).
- N.C. Gen. Stat. § 66-154(a): misappropriation "shall be permanently enjoined upon judgment finding misappropriation for the period that the trade secret exists plus an additional period as the court may deem necessary ... to eliminate any inequitable or unjust advantage". Under (a)(1), if an injunction is unreasonable, use may be conditioned on a reasonable royalty.
- *eBay Inc. v. MercExchange, L.L.C.*, 547 U.S. 388 (2006): four factors (irreparable injury, inadequate legal remedy, balance of hardships, public interest). Federal courts apply these to DTSA injunctions as well.
- *Apple Inc. v. Samsung Elecs. Co.*, 809 F.3d 633 (Fed. Cir. 2015): patent irreparable harm requires a causal nexus.
- *Robert Bosch LLC v. Pylon Mfg. Corp.*, 659 F.3d 1142 (Fed. Cir. 2011): there is no presumption of harm, but direct competition supports it.
- *Broadcom Corp. v. Qualcomm Inc.*, 543 F.3d 683, 704 (Fed. Cir. 2008): a sunset or transition period allows time for a design-around.
- *i4i Ltd. P'ship v. Microsoft Corp.*, 598 F.3d 831, 861–63 (Fed. Cir. 2010): a delayed effective date.
- *TiVo Inc. v. EchoStar Corp.*, 646 F.3d 869 (Fed. Cir. 2011) (en banc): redesigned products fall within an injunction only if they are "not more than colorably different" and still infringe.

**Answer.** The standards:
- **Patents:** eBay governs.
- **Trade secrets:** the DTSA is discretionary. The NCTSPA text is mandatory in form ("shall") but leaves room for royalty-conditioned use and ties the duration to the life of the secret plus a head-start period.

A defendant's claimed design-around cuts both ways. If credible, it:
- lowers the defendant's hardship (so it favors granting the injunction); but
- also weakens irreparable harm, because future infringement is less likely.

The injunction attaches to adjudicated products (D.I. 608-1 lists 19 part numbers) and to products not colorably different. Redesigned parts are litigated in contempt proceedings, not in the injunction order.

On 22 May 2024 Akoustis said it had "released these design updates to manufacturing for the remainder of its filter products" and that the updates have no "form, fit or function impact" (8-K Ex. 99.1). This supports a narrow patent injunction limited to the listed legacy parts.

The trade-secret prong of D.I. 608-1 is not tied to part numbers (quarantine, audits, and a use ban). For that prong the live questions are:
- duration: whether the head start was already compensated by the unjust-enrichment award;
- breadth: whether an injunction on "derived" information reaches the redesigned products.

**Disposition:** SETTLED on the standards. OPEN on scope and on how a court would treat the design-around.

## R2d: Nasdaq hearing stay and the second bid-price period (rules as of mid-2024)

**Citations:**
- Nasdaq Rules 5550(a)(2), 5810(c)(3)(A)(ii)–(iv), 5815(a)(1)(B), and 5815(c)(1)(A), as summarized in SEC Rel. No. 34-102245 (17 Jan 2025) at 2–3, which describes the pre-amendment rules:
  - a Capital Market company gets a second 180-day period if on day 180 it meets the continued-listing market value of publicly held shares requirement and all initial-listing standards except bid price, and notifies Nasdaq of its intent to cure;
  - if the deficiency is unresolved in the second period, Staff issues a Delisting Determination appealable to a Hearings Panel;
  - "A timely request for a hearing ordinarily stays the suspension of the security from trading pending the issuance of a written Hearings Panel decision";
  - the Panel "may ... allow a company up to an additional 180 days from the date of the Delisting Determination";
  - a trading-day closing bid of $0.10 or less for 10 consecutive trading days triggers an immediate Delisting Determination (5810(c)(3)(A)(iii));
  - reverse splits totalling 250:1 or more over the prior two years make the company ineligible for any compliance period (5810(c)(3)(A)(iv)).
- Akoustis 10-Q (13 May 2024): the second period expires **21 Oct 2024**; closing price $0.60 on 1 May 2024.

**Answer.** Under the rules in force at D:
1. If the $1.00 bid is not held for 10 consecutive business days by 21 Oct 2024, Staff issues a Delisting Determination.
2. A timely hearing request stays suspension until the Panel decides.
3. The Panel may extend to at most 180 days from the Determination, which is roughly April 2025.
4. A reverse split is the usual cure. A 10-day run at $0.10 or less short-circuits the process.

For the credit model, note that delisting is a Fundamental Change under the notes, with a holder put right (see `spec_corrections.md` item 7).

**Caveat (general law, post-D).** Nasdaq amended Rule 5815 by an order of 17 Jan 2025 (SR-NASDAQ-2024-045, filed 6 Aug 2024). After that change, a hearing request no longer stays suspension for a company that fails the second 180-day period. A D-dated model should use the mid-2024 rule above.

**Disposition:** SETTLED.

## R2e: Prejudgment interest on unjust-enrichment awards

**Citations:**
- N.C. Gen. Stat. § 24-5(b): "In an action other than contract, any portion of a money judgment designated by the fact finder as compensatory damages bears interest from the date the action is commenced until the judgment is satisfied." The legal rate is 8% under § 24-1.
- § 66-154(b): unjust enrichment is a measure of "actual damages".
- *Booker v. Taylor Milk Co.*, 64 F.3d 860, 868 (3d Cir. 1995): prejudgment interest on federal statutory claims is discretionary, with a presumption in favor "except where the award would result in 'unusual inequities'". The DTSA is silent on it.
- *General Motors Corp. v. Devex Corp.*, 461 U.S. 648 (1983): prejudgment interest is ordinarily awarded on patent damages under 35 U.S.C. § 284.
- *C&F Packing Co. v. IBP, Inc.*, 224 F.3d 1296 (Fed. Cir. 2000): prejudgment interest affirmed on a trade-secret award under state law.
- No N.C. appellate or Fourth Circuit decision was found squarely holding that § 24-5(b) applies, or does not apply, to an unjust-enrichment or disgorgement measure. That point is NOT FOUND.

**Answer.**
- **Exemplary damages:** no prejudgment interest. § 24-5(b) reaches only "compensatory damages". This part is SETTLED.
- **The $31.3M:** Qorvo will argue it is NCTSPA "actual damages", so § 24-5(b) interest is mandatory. Akoustis will argue three points:
  1. The jury designated the sum as "unjust enrichment", not "compensatory damages".
  2. Disgorgement is restitutionary; compare *Winant* in R2f.
  3. Bennis's figure is already a time-value computation. It present-values revenue to the trial date at a 14.8% WACC (Tr. 1695:25–1696:8; 1696:24–1697:11). Adding 8% from October 2021 would count the time value of money twice.

**Arithmetic, if interest is awarded** (8% simple, to the 20 May 2024 judgment):
- from filing, 4 Oct 2021 (959 days): about **$6.58M**;
- from the second amended complaint that added the trade-secret claims, 8 Feb 2023 (467 days): about **$3.21M**.

Whether the later claims relate back to the commencement date is a further sub-issue.

Post-judgment interest runs on the whole judgment under 28 U.S.C. § 1961.

**Disposition:** OPEN.

## R2f: UDTPA trebling of NCTSPA unjust enrichment

**Citations:**
- **D.I. 590 (14 May 2024), Order on Defendants' Rule 50(a) Motion re UDTPA damages (pre-D, this court):**
  - "North Carolina Courts have allowed for disgorgement of profits by defendants to serve as damages where they are proxies for injury by plaintiff, but has not recognized head start benefits to the Defendants as such a proxy."
  - "head start benefits are not a proxy for the damage to Plaintiff"
  - "The jury has no reasonable basis to find compensable damages under the UDTPA, but has sufficient basis to find injury." (pp. 3–5)
- N.C. Gen. Stat. § 75-16: "if damages are assessed ... judgment shall be rendered ... for treble the amount fixed by the verdict".
- *Winant v. Bostic*, 5 F.3d 767, 775–76 (4th Cir. 1993): § 75-16 does not allow trebling of a restitutionary award. Followed in *First South Bank v. Fifth Third Bank, N.A.* (4th Cir. 2015) (per curiam), per the Ellis & Winters blog, 9 Feb 2016.
- *Legacy Data Access, LLC v. MediQuant, Inc.* (W.D.N.C. 2017) (Whitney, C.J.): an NCTSPA violation is **not** a per se § 75-1.1 violation. The court declined to treble a $600k NCTSPA verdict where the jury had awarded $1 on the UDTPA claim. Per the Ellis & Winters blog, 9 Jan 2018.
- *Drouillard v. Keister Williams Newspaper Servs.*, 108 N.C. App. 169 (1992): no automatic violation. *Med. Staffing Network v. Ridgway*, 194 N.C. App. 649 (2009), contains contrary language ("violation of the Trade Secrets Protection Act constitutes an unfair act or practice"). *Grout Doctor* (E.D.N.C. 2015) is cited by Qorvo in D.I. 587 for "as a matter of law".
- *Hardy v. Toler*, 288 N.C. 303 (1975): the jury finds the facts, and the court decides as a matter of law whether they amount to an unfair practice.
- *United Labs., Inc. v. Kuykendall*, 335 N.C. 183 (1993): a plaintiff may not recover both punitive and treble damages for the same conduct and must elect.

**Answer.** D.I. 611 asks the court to amend the jury's "No" on Question 2(a) and to treble the $31,315,215 to $93,945,645. The pre-D record already contains the court's ruling on the key premise. The court told the jury that head-start unjust enrichment is not a cognizable UDTPA damages measure (D.I. 590). § 75-16 trebles only "damages" for injury to the plaintiff.

Three independent obstacles:
1. **No per se rule.** NCTSPA misappropriation is not per se a UDTPA violation. The authorities are split at the Court of Appeals level, and *Legacy Data* is the closest federal analog.
2. **No trebling of restitution.** The Fourth Circuit bars trebling restitutionary awards (*Winant*).
3. **Election.** If trebling were granted, N.C. election doctrine would force a choice between trebled damages and the NCTSPA punitive award. Qorvo's own proposed order describes the $93.9M as "compensatory damages ... in aggregate".

Qorvo's best route:
- *Hardy v. Toler*: unfairness is a legal question the court may decide on the jury's facts;
- the willful-and-malicious finding;
- *Ridgway* and *Grout Doctor*.

Even on that route, Qorvo still needs a cognizable damages measure, which D.I. 590 held was absent.

There is also a procedural obstacle. Qorvo proceeds under Rule 59(e) (D.I. 611). The 17 Jun filings include no Qorvo Rule 50(b) motion, and the only JMOL motion is Akoustis's (D.I. 607). Setting aside a jury finding normally requires the Rule 50 route (*Unitherm Food Sys. v. Swift-Eckrich*, 546 U.S. 394 (2006)). Qorvo will answer that § 75-1.1 unfairness is a question of law for the court (*Hardy*).

The court's own pre-D ruling makes grant unlikely, but the question remains formally open.

**Disposition:** OPEN, leaning strongly against trebling.

## R2g: Attorney fees under DTSA § 1836(b)(3)(D) and NCTSPA § 66-154(d)

**Citations:**
- 18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)(3)(D): the court "may ... if ... the trade secret was willfully and maliciously misappropriated, award reasonable attorney's fees to the prevailing party".
- N.C. Gen. Stat. § 66-154(d): "the court may award reasonable attorneys' fees".
- *Bruning & Federle Mfg. Co. v. Mills*, 185 N.C. App. 153 (2007): the award is discretionary even when the statutory conditions are met.
- *Proofpoint, Inc. v. Vade Secure, Inc.*, No. 19-cv-04238-MMC, D.I. 905 (N.D. Cal. 5 Sep 2023): the court **denied** fees despite a jury finding of willful and malicious misappropriation and $13.5M in unjust enrichment. It had also denied exemplary damages. The court noted that the cases awarding fees had also awarded exemplary damages.
- *Syntel Sterling Best Shores Mauritius Ltd. v. TriZetto Grp.*, No. 15-cv-211 (S.D.N.Y.):
  - an earlier award of $14.5M in fees (Mondaq, 17 Apr 2024);
  - a later order (2025/26) awarding $12,395,484.50, a 30% reduction from $17,707,835 requested.
- *Motorola Sols. v. Hytera* (N.D. Ill.): fees awarded. The UK enforcement judgment ([2024] EWHC 2891) recites $34.2M in fees in the U.S. judgment.

**Answer.** A willful-and-malicious finding opens the door, and the court then exercises discretion. Here the jury also awarded exemplary damages ($7M), and courts usually pair exemplary awards with fee awards (*Proofpoint*'s observation). That makes an award more likely than a denial.

Amounts are routinely cut through lodestar review. Deductions are typically for:
- rates and block billing;
- work on unsuccessful claims (here: the RICO and false-marking claims were dismissed at summary judgment, and 104 trade secrets were reduced to 46, of which 37 were found);
- patent-only work, which is not recoverable under the trade-secret fee statutes without a § 285 "exceptional case" finding.

No systematic dataset of awarded-to-requested ratios was found. From the anecdotes, a bounded prior is that awards run about 50–90% of the request when granted, and outright denial is possible. On the $12,116,123.30 request (D.I. 618), that implies roughly $0 to $11M, with a central tendency of about $7–10M. This range is inference, not data.

**Disposition:** SETTLED on the discretionary standard. OPEN on outcome.

Default bond: the full judgment of $38.6M plus post-judgment interest and costs, possibly plus estimated fees. A reduced or alternative security requires a showing of good cause (the *Dillon*/*Poplar Grove* line).

Docket check: entries 1–621, all filed on or before 17 Jun 2024, contain no stay order, bond, or supersedeas motion. After the judgment, the docket shows only:
- the post-trial briefing stipulation (D.I. 604/605);
- a transcript redaction (D.I. 606);

So from 20 Jun 2024 the judgment was enforceable and unstayed.

**Disposition:** SETTLED.

## R2i: Surety collateral for a distressed judgment debtor

**Citations:**
- Court Surety, "How Much Do You Have to Put Up for an Appeal Bond?" (10 Apr 2023): when a debtor doesn't meet the surety's qualifications, "generally they require collateral in the full amount of the bond". Less than 100% is accepted only "on rare occasions when the judgment debtor is very qualified". The example given: a company with cash equal to the bond would still be required to post collateral.
- Court Surety, "Using Letters of Credit to Secure Appeal Bonds" (2022): the accepted forms are cash and bank letters of credit, with real estate and securities at higher cost.
- Premiums are commonly cited at 1–2% of the bond per year (industry practice, anecdotal).
- Akoustis 10-Q (13 May 2024): cash of $15.2M; going-concern doubt.

**Answer.** An issuer with going-concern doubt and cash below the bond amount would face a demand for about 100% collateral (cash or a letter of credit) to bond a $38.6M+ judgment. At 13 May 2024 it lacked that cash. A bond is therefore practically unavailable without new money, which makes a court-approved alternative security, or a stay application, the realistic path.

**Disposition:** OPEN (bounded; industry anecdote, not court data). Treat as: collateral required ≈ 100% of the bond, with a range of 80–110%.

## R2j: Indenture "final judgment" (2022 notes; N.Y. law)

**Governing law** (§ 17.10, verbatim): "THE INTERNAL LAW OF THE STATE OF NEW YORK SHALL GOVERN AND BE USED TO CONSTRUE THIS INDENTURE, THE NOTES AND THE NOTE GUARANTEE WITHOUT REGARD TO PRINCIPLES OF CONFLICTS OF LAW THAT WOULD APPLY ANY OTHER LAW." The Guarantee is also governed by New York law.

**§ 7.01(i), verbatim, including the insured-amounts exclusion:** "a final judgment or final judgments for the payment of money are entered by a court or courts of competent jurisdiction against the Company or any of its Subsidiaries and such judgment or judgments remain undischarged, unpaid or unstayed for a period (during which execution shall not be effectively stayed) of 60 days, provided that the aggregate of all such undischarged judgments exceeds $10.0 million (excluding amounts covered by insurance), after notice to the Company by the Trustee or the Holders of at least 25% in aggregate Principal Amount of the Notes then outstanding voting as a single class;"

**§ 7.02, rescission text, verbatim:** "The Majority Holders by written notice to the Trustee may, on behalf of all of the Holders, rescind an acceleration and its consequences if the rescission would not conflict with any judgment or decree and if all existing Events of Default (except nonpayment of Principal, Interest or premium that has become due solely because of the acceleration) have been cured or waived."

The automatic-rescission paragraph for cross-default under § 7.01(h) reads: "the declaration of acceleration of the Notes shall be automatically rescinded and annulled if the payment default or acceleration triggering such Event of Default pursuant to Section 7.01(h) shall be remedied or cured or waived by the holders of the relevant debt within 20 Business Days after the declaration of acceleration with respect thereto and if the rescission and annulment of the acceleration of the Notes would not conflict with any judgment or decree of a court of competent jurisdiction obtained by the Trustee for the payment of amounts due on the Notes."

Acceleration for (i) is optional: the Trustee or 25% of holders may declare it.

**Citations:**
- *Beal Sav. Bank v. Sommer*, 8 N.Y.3d 318, 324–25 (2007): read the contract as a whole, by its plain meaning.
- *Sharon Steel Corp. v. Chase Manhattan Bank*, 691 F.2d 1039, 1048 (2d Cir. 1982): boilerplate indenture terms are construed uniformly, as a matter of law.
- Fed. R. Civ. P. 54(a), 58, 62(a); Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(4)(A): timely Rule 50(b)/59 motions suspend the appeal clock, but not enforceability after 30 days.

**Answer.** The clause does not say "final and non-appealable". It measures default by whether the judgment is "undischarged, unpaid or unstayed" and whether "execution shall ... be effectively stayed". That structure only makes sense for judgments that remain subject to challenge, so appealability does not defeat finality. The better reading is that the Rule 58 judgment of 20 May 2024, a "final judgment for the payment of money", qualifies even while Rule 50/59 motions are pending.

Akoustis's counter-argument: a judgment whose amount may still be remitted, trebled, or supplemented with interest and fees is not yet "final", because appellate finality is suspended.

No N.Y. decision on this precise point was found.

Mechanics if it qualifies:

**How the award scales with months (inference).** Bennis's model is approximately:

benefit(m) = PV_actual × (1 − 1.148^(−m/12))

As a check, 140 × (1 − 1.148^(−55/12)) = 65.6, against the $66.1M testified.

- about **21.8 months** of head start on the $66.1M base: 12 months ≈ $18.2M, 18 ≈ $26.4M, 22 ≈ $31.5M, 24 ≈ $34.0M;
- about **30.0 months** on the $50.3M base: 24 months ≈ $25.9M, 30 ≈ $31.3M.

The 22-month match to Shanfield's single "22 months" item is suggestive but unproven. The jury found 37 of the 46 trade secrets presented, and the verdict form did not ask for months.

**Disposition:** SETTLED (record quotations). The implied-months figures are labelled inference.
