# Slope's lending sizing and practices, from public sources

Compiled 25 Sep 2026 from Slope's own help center, product pages and press releases, plus two news reports. These describe current practice (help-center articles edited 2024–2025; the Amazon program dates from December 2025). Applying them to a historical decision date is a reconstruction: it asks what Slope, as it describes itself, would offer this business on D.

## Who and what Slope finances

- **Who.** B2B wholesalers, manufacturers and distributors; B2B retail; B2B and B2C marketplace sellers (Amazon, Walmart, Alibaba); invoicing and bill-payment platforms; B2B service providers. Industry is not an eligibility test. [P3, P1]
- **What.** Bill pay (Pay Later) finances "on an unsecured basis) revenue-generating invoices, such as inventory purchases and marketing spend". Slope pays the vendor within one business day. [H1]
- **Lender of record.** Loans are made by Lead Bank. The Amazon program is funded through a J.P. Morgan credit facility. Slope reports more than $500M of SMB originations on its own capital. [H1, P4, P6]

## Eligibility [H2, H3]

- At least 1 year in business and of banking history.
- At least 3 years in business for lines above $100k.
- Annual revenue of $100k or more.
- A personal guarantee is required below $5M of annual revenue.
- An owner FICO of about 625.
- **No bankruptcies in the last 5 years.**
- All business bank accounts connected through Plaid. Bank data is "core to our credit decision" and drives the limit, the pricing and eligibility.
- Factors considered: bank-account activity, revenue, leverage, business credit history and the owner's personal credit.

## Line size [H1, H3, H5, P4, N1]

- **Rule:** "Financing limits typically range from ~15% to approximately a third of your monthly revenue after debt service, depending on your credit profile."
- **Two limits:** a pre-approved line limit, and an order limit, which is the largest single financed transaction.
- **Approval:** automated up to $250k in the bill-pay flow (up to $100k in the DocuSketch flow). Larger lines need manual review and more information.
- **Upper end:** up to $5M in the Amazon program, "for the strongest profiles".
- **Reassessment:** lines are "reassess[ed] ... from time to time" and "can automatically upsize based on cash flow trends". SlopeScore describes dynamic line sizing that adjusts with real-time cash-flow data to prevent over-extension.

## Terms [H1, H4, F1, P4]

- **Bill pay:** net terms up to 90 days, or monthly installments up to 120 days. Net 90 and 120-day installments are for selected customers only.
- **Common installment option:** 2 equal payments over 60 days (day 30 and 60), or 3 equal payments over 90 days (day 30, 60 and 90).
- **Checkout FAQ:** 30 and 60-day net terms, or 60 and 90-day installments.
- **Amazon line of credit:** revolving; the borrower picks a 2–12 month term for each draw.

## Price [H1, M1, N1, P4, F2]

- Risk-based.
- As low as 0.99% for Net 30 and 2.99% for Net 90 for the best customers ("below 12% APR"). The bill-pay page says "as low as 1.60% on Net 30".
- Bloomberg reports Slope "often charges about 2.5% on a 60-day loan, ... about 15%" annualized.
- The Amazon line starts at 8.99% APR.
- No fee to apply or to keep a line open.
- Fees are prorated on early full repayment.
- Card repayment carries a 3.05% processing fee.
- "We do impose interest if you default on the loan."

## Repayment [H6, H7]

- The chosen payment method is debited automatically on each due date. Reminders start at least a week before.
- ACH repayments take about 3 business days to clear and free up the limit.
- As the line is repaid, capacity frees up for the next draw.

## What this means for case selection and the demo

- **The loan is sized from the borrower.**
  - Line = 15–33% of monthly revenue after debt service.
  - A stressed borrower sits at the low end of that range.
  - A single draw is one real supplier invoice, capped by the order limit.
  - Rough scale:
    - $100k–$250k lines (automated approval) fit roughly $4M–$20M of annual revenue.
    - $0.5M–$2M lines fit roughly $20M–$160M, before debt service.
  - The demo's $2M is at the manual-review top of the bill-pay range.
- **Revenue floor.** $100k of annual revenue is Slope's floor. The $10M floor in our criteria was ours, not Slope's.
- **Industry.** A business that buys inventory, components or marketing on invoices qualifies, whatever it makes.
- **Price at our kind of borrower.** For the stressed borrower the demo needs, the realistic price is the upper part of the range. The supplied 3.7% over 3 monthly installments is about 22% APR on the average balance, which is consistent.
- **Bankruptcy.** A bankruptcy in the 5 years before D makes the borrower ineligible.
- **The lender relationship is a reusable line, not one loan.** Draws are repaid over 30–120 days, and the line is reassessed on bank data. A lender-side channel follows from this: when cash flow deteriorates, the line is resized and exposure falls on later draws. That is how Slope itself would respond to the event.
